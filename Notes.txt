Query 1 — Weak Auth Check

sql
SELECT user_id, department, auth_type
FROM fake_logins
WHERE auth_type = 'basic';

What it does: Shows every login that used basic username/password instead of SSO/key/token. These are the weakest, most outdated logins — a security risk because Paul mentioned companies are moving away from basic auth.






Query 2 — Repeated Failed Logins

sql
SELECT user_id, COUNT(*) AS failed_attempts
FROM fake_logins
WHERE success = 'false'
GROUP BY user_id
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 5
ORDER BY failed_attempts DESC;

What it does: Finds users who failed to log in 5+ times, sorted worst first. Could indicate a locked-out account or someone trying to break in.







Query 3 — Odd-Hour Logins

sql
SELECT user_id, department, timestamp, auth_type
FROM fake_logins
WHERE CAST(strftime('%H', timestamp) AS INTEGER) < 6
   OR CAST(strftime('%H', timestamp) AS INTEGER) > 21
ORDER BY timestamp;

What it does: Finds logins that happened before 6am or after 9pm — outside normal working hours, which could indicate suspicious access.

This one's a bit more advanced than the first two — don't worry if strftime feels unfamiliar, it's just SQLite's built-in way of pulling pieces out of a date/time value. You don't need to memorize the syntax, just understand what it's doing: extracting the hour and checking if it's outside normal working hours.




Query 4 — Unusual System Access

sql
SELECT user_id, department, system_accessed, COUNT(*) AS times_accessed
FROM fake_logins
GROUP BY user_id, system_accessed
ORDER BY user_id, times_accessed;

What it does: Shows how many times each person accessed each system. Low counts next to a person's name (1-2 times) next to systems they don't normally use are worth flagging as unusual access.


What to look for: scroll through and look for users who have one system with a high count (their normal one) and a different system with a count of just 1 or 2 — that low number is the "unusual access" signal. This query doesn't auto-flag it for you like the others; it gives you the raw breakdown, and you interpret it. That's actually a good thing to mention later — it shows you understand the difference between a query that filters automatically vs one you have to read and reason about.
